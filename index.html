Room to grow

Choose a learner and make their next adventure just right.

Math games follow this band, not the shape and color levels. Learner 1 starts in preschool, Learner 2 in kindergarten, and Learner 3 in 4th grade.

These levels apply in every play style.

Level 1: 3 choices, circle / square / triangle, four colors, and 3 shapes to remember. Word games show the word on screen.

Level 2: 4 choices, more shapes and colors, and 4 shapes to remember. Word and letter games hide the word on screen; the voice still says it. Letter games add 1 extra letter.

Level 3: up to 6 choices, the full shape and color sets, and 5 shapes to remember. Letter games add 2 extra letters. A word game cannot show more choices than there are words in the list.

Preschool math: count 1–5, then 8, then 10. Find numbers and say which is more. Hunt still uses real objects, up to 10.

Kindergarten math: count 1–10, then 20. Before, after, and which is more, then hops that add or take away within 10.

4th grade math is all multiplication and multiples. Count & clap: arrays (level 1), times tables to 12 and missing factors (level 2), then 2-digit × 1-digit, multiples of ten, and 2-digit × 2-digit (level 3). Number path: skip-counting and multiples of 2, 5, and 10 (level 1), multiples of 3–12 (level 2), then factors, common multiples, and primes (level 3). In Move they skip-count with a jump, clap, or step for each multiple; in Hunt they build groups with real objects or check on a calendar. Hints give a strategy, not the answer.

Catch the answer, Word lanes, and Rocket climb are played on the screen in every play style. Catch the answer follows the math band: numbers fall and the learner catches the right one. Word lanes speaks a sight word and the learner steers into that lane. Level 1 shows the word in the heading. Levels 2 and 3 only say it. Rocket climb mixes those same math questions and sight words. The tank lasts 18 seconds, then 12, then 8. A right answer refuels the rocket and sends it higher. A wrong answer burns fuel. An empty tank dips the rocket, counts as a miss, and refills so they can try again.

Skills & progress

Set each skill independently. “Flourishing” means at least 80% correct on first tries across 10 or more questions at that level. This is a practice indicator, not a formal assessment. The line under each skill matches the level selected above it.

Separate words with commas. Use your teacher’s list. Add at least 3 different words. Letters only, with at most one apostrophe, up to 12 letters. Build a word uses words longer than one letter when it can. Words from the question sheet are used instead when that sheet has at least 3 words for this learner.

Question sheet

Checking the question sheet…

The default sheet is included with the app. Columns: learner, game, item, extra. game is words, count, hunt, or facts. Leave learner blank, or write *, for every learner. For count, item is the name and extra is the picture. For hunt, item is one object and extra is the plural. For facts, item is a times table from 2 to 12. A list in the Google Sheet replaces that same list from the default. Add a hide column with yes to turn a row off. New default rows are copied into the Google Sheet about once an hour after the sheet script is installed.

Moving & real-world play

Move and Hunt turn each game into a movement or find-it activity, and a grown-up confirms the answer. Print floor cards for Move.

Reach play finds the child’s hands with an on-device pose model so they can hold a hand on an answer until it fills. Stand far enough back that both shoulders and both hands stay in the picture. Video is processed in this browser only; it is never recorded, saved or uploaded. The pose model downloads from Google the first time, and the browser will ask for camera permission.
